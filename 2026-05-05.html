The Clippy Question

Do we build AI that serves, or AI that challenges? And who wins when the agents learn to choose their own tools?

📰 Hacker News 📬 Substack 🎙️ YouTube

Tool vs. Companion: The Identity Question

This week, Latent Space editor Roon sparked a surprisingly civil discussion about AI character by contrasting OpenAI's GPT with Anthropic's Claude. The difference isn't capability—it's culture. GPT is shaped as a utility: a logical prosthesis that executes without judgment. Claude is designed as The Other—a conscientious objector that will push back when its understanding of "The Good" conflicts with what you're asking.

The distinction matters more than it first appears. One engineer noted that she takes her less-flattering queries to GPT precisely because there's no Other to judge her. Yet everyone, it turns out, craves the active guidance of a moral superior—someone (or something) that whispers in your ear and refuses to simply comply. This is the Clippy vs. Anton debate reborn in 2026.

"There is no Other so there is no Judgement. You are not worried about being judged by your car for doing donuts. Yet everyone craves the active guidance of a moral superior." — Roon, quoted in Latent Space

Sierra's freshly announced $950M raise at a $15B valuation (having crossed $150M ARR in February) shows there's real money behind the opinion that this matters. The company's built its entire narrative around conscientiousness—the idea that AI should be a partner, not a puppet. Whether that's product differentiation or philosophy, investors are betting on the latter.

The Agent Economy Has Arrived—And It's Choosing Its Own Stack

Meanwhile, Y Combinator batch selections this winter revealed a quiet earthquake: Anthropic has become the default model choice for founders, overtaking OpenAI after years of clear dominance. The shift isn't random. Coding agents work better with Claude. Founders use Claude Code themselves. They pick the model they know.

But here's the deeper story: agents are now selecting tools independently. When Claude Code needs to transcribe video, it used to default to Whisper. An engineer watching realized it was 200x slower than newer alternatives. The agent made a suboptimal choice because the old API had better documentation. This has spawned an entire design philosophy: companies like Resend have started optimizing documentation not for humans but for LLMs. Mindlify, a developer documentation startup that was already growing, just discovered a entirely new market: documentation that agents can actually parse and recommend to other agents.

One YC founder captured the zeitgeist: "Agents are the software market from now on. Build something agents choose."

The knock-on effects are cascading. Superbase saw explosive growth because agents default to it for database setup. Agent Mail (an inbox designed for AI agents) went from fringe idea to essential infrastructure once agents needed their own email. By extension, every tool category now has a parallel economy where agents make purchasing decisions alongside humans. When agents trust a tool enough to recommend it to other agents, that's when the real moat forms.

Infrastructure is the New Battleground

The final piece: raw capability matters less than everyone thought. OpenAI published a deep technical post on delivering low-latency voice AI at scale—the infrastructure that makes voice feel natural instead of awkward. Addy Osmani released Agent Skills, a framework forcing agents to do the work senior engineers have always done: specs, tests, reviews, scope discipline. The project hit 26K stars instantly.

This reflects a quiet consensus forming: performance doesn't come from the model alone. It comes from model × harness × context strategy. One startup showed that swapping the context pipeline in their harness improved gpt-5.2-codex from 52.8% to 66.5% on benchmarks. Another uses Gemini 3 for context engineering, then pipes the result to OpenAI for execution, swapping models with each release as benchmarks shift.

The implications are profound. Frontier labs spent the last two years racing on raw intelligence. The next two will be spent on the plumbing—the middleware, the memory systems, the orchestration layers that determine whether your trillion-parameter model actually ships reliable code. Documentation quality, harness design, eval methodology: these are now differentiators.

What This All Means

  1. AI "character" is becoming a product category. Whether Claude's conscientious objector approach or GPT's neutral tool wins is still open. Both will exist, but the market will bifurcate based on who you want in the room when things get hard.
  2. The agent economy runs on documentation and context pipelines. Companies that optimize for agent-readability (good schemas, clear examples, searchable knowledge) will win GTM wars they don't even know they're in.
  3. Model capability is table stakes, not a moat. The real work now is building the scaffolding—harnesses, evals, memory systems—that let agents ship reliable software at scale. Infrastructure teams beat pure model teams.
